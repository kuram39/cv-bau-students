# Design philosophy — "Deterministic Cartography"

A visual worldview for mapping a system where two kinds of work coexist: the
**expensive, irreversible gesture** and the **free, infinitely repeatable
operation**. The poster is a map of that distinction — drawn with the patient,
clinical hand of a master cartographer who has spent countless hours making a
complex flow look inevitable.

**Space and form.** A calm, paper-pale field divided by an invisible grid.
Process steps are rendered as precise, rounded capsules — uniform, weighted,
placed with surveyor's discipline. Flow reads in lanes, like a transit map: each
lane a role, each capsule a station, each connector a quiet directional gesture.
Nothing decorative; every line earns its place. Generous margins let the system
breathe, so the eye is led rather than crowded — the product of painstaking
spatial editing.

**Color and material.** Two pigments carry all the meaning, calibrated like
Albers studied his squares. A deep **ink-blue** marks the costly cognitive act
(the language model — it thinks, it costs, it runs once). A grounded **field-green**
marks the deterministic machine (it computes, it is free, it repeats forever).
Colour is the legend; the viewer learns the rule in one glance and then reads the
whole map fluently. A near-black ink for structure, a warm off-white for the
ground — restraint as sophistication.

**Scale and rhythm.** One commanding title set in a confident condensed grotesque,
then a steep drop to small, clinical labels — the typographic silence of a
scientific plate. Repetition builds meaning: capsules of equal height, connectors
of equal weight, a steady cadence broken only where the system itself breaks
(the single emphasised idea: *expensive once → free forever*). The rhythm rewards
sustained viewing the way a well-kept ledger does.

**Composition and hierarchy.** Three movements left-to-right — origin, core,
oversight — resolved by a single anchored statement at the foot, the thesis the
whole map exists to prove. Visual weight flows toward that line. Every alignment
is the residue of many refinements; every capsule sits on the grid as if it could
sit nowhere else. The composition must look meticulously crafted, master-level,
laboured over with care — an artefact that makes an ephemeral architectural truth
feel observable, mapped, and understood.
