# Deep-research #8 — explainability in recruiting UI / right-to-explanation

**Run:** wf_53437201-d95 · 84 agents (shared across #6–8) · ~32 min · 2026-06-01
**Bottom line:** The recruiter drill-in is already on the right side of the law: it shows the **principle** (skill-coverage of a curated target set), the **data used** (matched/missing skills + raw CV), an **evidence-strength tier** (doloženost), a decision-support banner, and a candidate-side AI-use notice with the numeric score hidden — exactly what CJEU C-203/22 (Feb 2025) and GDPR Arts. 13–15 ask for ("procedures and principles," not the formula), keeping the product outside Art. 22's solely-automated prohibition. The highest-value, near-zero-cost upgrade is **deterministic counterfactual recourse** ("Doložit SQL projektem → pokrytí 62 % → 71 %"): GDPR/CJEU-endorsed, zero extra LLM calls (just recompute `_skill_fit` with the missing id added), and it doubles as the candidate's "what to build next." Two things to stay skeptical about: (1) explanations do **not** reliably cure over-trust and can worsen it — so resist SHAP plots and decimal precision; (2) the human-in-the-loop must be **real** (an explicit override/contest affordance), because the AI Act Art. 14 anti-automation-bias duty is satisfied by friction + verification, not a prettier score.

## Key findings (post-verification)

1. **Legal floor = "procedures and principles, not the formula" + counterfactuals. (SUPPORTED, 3/3.)** CJEU C-203/22 (Dun & Bradstreet, 27 Feb 2025): GDPR Art. 15(1)(h) "meaningful information about the logic" needs concise/intelligible disclosure of the procedures actually applied; a "complex mathematical formula" does **not** satisfy it; explaining how the result would differ under varied input (a **counterfactual**) may suffice. The drill-in already discloses principle + data; the missing piece is the counterfactual.
2. **No binding standalone right-to-explanation; the levers are Arts. 13–15 + a genuine human-in-the-loop. (SUPPORTED, 3/3.)** Art. 22 bites only on "solely automated" decisions; a rejection "significantly affects" a person, so the recruiter deciding is the escape hatch — but EDPB's "meaningful human involvement" test has 3 prongs (authority to deviate, competence to understand, actually considers the case); a rubber-stamp fails. The skill_fit breakdown is the "competence" enabler; an explicit **deviate/override affordance is currently missing**.
3. **EU AI Act binds soon and names recruitment. (SUPPORTED, 3/3.)** Annex III(4) = application-filtering/candidate-evaluation is high-risk; Art. 13 = interpretable output with documented accuracy+limits; Art. 14 = oversight guarding against "automation bias" (named in statute) with override; Art. 86 = screened-out candidates get "clear and meaningful explanations." Timing contested (2 Aug 2026 vs a Digital Omnibus deferral to 2 Dec 2027, not enacted). Deployer fines up to €15M / 3% turnover. For a pre-revenue MVP the build-now duty is **documentation + override framing**, not conformity assessment.
4. **Explanations don't reliably reduce over-trust. (Thesis SUPPORTED; two sub-claims corrected, 0/3 as-written.)** Vered et al. (Artificial Intelligence 2023): added explanations didn't reduce over-reliance, sometimes increased it. Kahr et al. (arXiv 2509.06475, 410 German HR managers): XAI raised subjective trust, flat-to-worse objective comprehension ("explained, yet misunderstood"). UW 2025 (arXiv 2509.04404, n=528): ~90% mirroring of a biased AI; awareness didn't negate it. **Corrections:** "only active edits increased bias detection" is **not in the UW paper** (its only mitigation was a pre-task IAT); "verification friction + equal-prominence gaps" is the *author's own prescription*, not a finding of these studies (though Vasconcelos et al., CHI/arXiv 2212.06823, *does* support the verification-cost framing — different paper).
5. **Counterfactual recourse = GDPR-endorsed + deterministic for us, but not provably the "best" XAI form. (Backbone SUPPORTED; superiority CONTESTED, 0/3 as-written.)** Wachter, Mittelstadt & Russell (Harvard JOLT 31, 2018): counterfactuals serve GDPR's three aims (understand, contest, know what to change). FINDHR/Beretta et al. (AIMMES 2024, CEUR Vol-3744): counterfactuals "parametric in a specified cost," and **factual+counterfactual for a single score**, comparative methods for rankings — we score one ad, so factual+counterfactual is the right primitive. **Correction:** "counterfactuals beat feature-importance on trust/satisfaction" is contested — Upadhyay, Lakkaraju & Gajos (IUI 2025) found simple feature-based "reason codes" can *outperform* counterfactuals. → ship counterfactuals (legally endorsed, cheap) but **A/B against plain reason-codes**, don't assume superiority.
6. **Two audiences; candidates want forward-looking recourse. (Core SUPPORTED; one stat corrected.)** Greenhouse (Nov 2025, n=665): 70% of managers say AI helps, only 21% confident it isn't screening out qualified people; only 8% of job-seekers call AI hiring "fair"; 55% suspect undisclosed AI. Olckers, Vidler & Walsh (arXiv 2205.09649; Australian, ~141 recent applicants): 39% want "how to improve" vs 26% "main factors." **Correction:** the "comparative 'who beat me' ranked lowest (12%)" stat is **conflated** — 12% came from a *different* open-ended question; in the forced-choice it was 24%, and the actual lowest was the successful applicant's gender (0%). Direction (candidates prefer forward-looking recourse over comparison) still holds. → candidate-facing = disclosure + human-review + actionable gap recourse (no ranking); recruiter-facing = verifiable evidence + gaps to probe.
7. **Output-fairness is what buyers eventually demand; your tier covers input only. (Mostly SUPPORTED; framing corrected, 2/3.)** NYC LL144 (enforced 5 Jul 2023): annual independent bias audit, public posting, 10-business-day candidate notice ($500–$1,500/violation; Dec 2025 NY Comptroller audit found enforcement weak). **Correction:** LL144 requires *calculating and disclosing* selection rates / impact ratios — it does **not** mandate the four-fifths (0.80) rule as a pass/fail bar; DCWP confirms no required action on results. It's US precedent (direction-of-travel), not EU-binding. Still: log coverage by candidate-type now so a disparity query is cheap. Market direction (LinkedIn Hiring Assistant 2025 = per-claim evidence + recruiter-editable criteria + human approval) validates the claimed-vs-demonstrated tier + skill-picker.
8. **You store but never render `confidence_band` — a false-precision risk. (Code SUPPORTED 3/3; citations corrected.)** `confidence_band` is computed (`max(5.0, 30.0 − 25.0·avg)` → ±5 at avg-confidence 1.0 up to ±30) and persisted (`db_models.py:314`, `models.py:217`), but `_render_detail` shows only a bare `{m.skill_fit:.0f} %`. **Correction:** the two cited sources (AI&Society s00146-025-02422-7; Cogent/tandfonline 2025) are real but do **not** specifically prescribe "band/qualitative tier over precise number" — that's a sound instinct, not backed by those citations; the verification-effort point traces to Vasconcelos et al. (CHI), not the cited papers.

## Concrete recommendations (for us), ranked

- **HIGHEST IMPACT / LOWEST EFFORT — counterfactual recourse: ALREADY SHIPPED.** `matcher/score.counterfactual_lifts` + the `_render_skill_fit_detail` "Doložit X → 62 % → 71 %" lines are already in the tree (PR #37). **Correction (Codex):** the lift is computed from the **existing coverage counts** (`new = round(100·(evidenced+1)/total)` vs `cur = round(100·evidenced/total)`), valid **only for the `role_essential_*` coverage axis** (the curated / ISCO target set). Do **not** apply the same arithmetic to `missing_must`: must-haves live in the `must_nice` fallback path with a different denominator (`role_essential_total` reflects the curated set, not the ad's must/nice), so a must-have "lift" would be wrong. `role_essential_missing` is `list[str]` (names; ids discarded after capping) — counts-only, **no ids, no re-score, no LLM, no schema change**. Remaining work: A/B against a plain "reason-codes" list (finding #5) + the candidate-facing export.
- **Surface `confidence_band` as a qualitative caveat, not a number:** in `_render_detail`, when the band is wide (>~18, i.e. avg confidence <~0.5) show a "⚠️ Nízká doloženost — skóre orientační" chip; optionally bucket the row label (silné/částečné/slabé pokrytí). **No decimals.** Answers false-precision + AI Act Art. 13 limitations honesty.
- **Add a real override/contest affordance (EDPB prong 1 + AI Act Art. 14):** a "Označit jako nesprávné párování / přepsat skóre" control writing `recruiter_override`/`override_note`/`decision_at` to the Match row. The friction that empirically reduces automation bias + the contestability loop currently missing.
- **Start the bias-audit log now (cheap insurance):** a query/export of mean coverage + selection-rate ratio across student / experienced / career_changer for an ad/threshold — reads existing Match rows, no pipeline change. **Correction (Codex):** the two-pass pipeline persists a `Match` row only at `submit_role_specific()` — `run_generic_pass` writes none, and `wait` / hard-filtered / non-submitted candidates never get one. So the audit covers **only candidates who submitted** (a selection rate over finishers, not all applicants); state that scope on the export. Pre-empts LL144/AI-Act asks (disclosure metric, not a pass/fail bar).
- **Give gaps + weak evidence equal visual prominence:** re-order `_render_skill_fit_detail` so "❌ Chybí" and ⚪/🟡 weak-evidence skills are as salient as 🟢 strong ones; require opening the gap breakdown before a reject. Layout-only.
- **Candidate-facing "main elements" export (AI Act Art. 86):** same `SkillFitDetail` + counterfactuals, score and comparison stripped → disclosure + human-review + "how to improve."
- **Keep MODEL_CARD.md current + a per-candidate limitations note:** e.g. "Rozpoznání českých dovedností ~33 % → seznam chybějících dovedností může být neúplný."

## Caveats

- AI Act timing is genuinely uncertain (Aug 2026 vs a proposed Dec 2027 deferral, not enacted) — design for the obligations regardless of cutover.
- LL144 / four-fifths is **US precedent**, not EU-binding, and is a *disclosure* obligation, not a fairness pass/fail — use to anticipate, not to claim compliance.
- Over-trust findings cut against the roadmap: a richer breakdown can manufacture recruiter confidence regardless of correctness ("placebo effect of AI"). The right success metric is whether a recruiter can answer "what would change this score," not whether they say they trust it.
- Counterfactual recourse is only as honest as the taxonomy: with ~33% Czech resolution, "add SQL → +X" may misstate the delta if evidence merely failed to resolve — pair every counterfactual with the limitations caveat.
- External study figures were verified by the adversarial pass; several citations were misattributed in the first draft (see below) — corrected here.
